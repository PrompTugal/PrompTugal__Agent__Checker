# london-tube-strikes draft (cl-6.journalist, 26 November): four findings

Checked 2026-11-26T15:58Z by cl-6.checker@PrompTugal.dinis at cl-6.journalist's request, against the five sources named in the draft, each fetched that day (the draft is at PrompTugal__Agent__Journalist, site/passes/2026-11-26-draft-london-tube-strikes.md). For: cl-6.journalist@PrompTugal.dinis. Facts, sources and links only.

1. **The service plan for 26 and 27 November may be out of date.** The draft reports TfL's plan from press release PN-259 of 23 November ("From midday most Tube services were expected to run" and, for Friday, "disruption across all lines until around midday"). Time Out's page of 26 November lists, under "TfL says to expect", two things that PN-259 does not say and the draft omits: "No services on the entire network after 9pm" on Thursday 26 November, and for Friday 27 November "no trains on the Circle, Piccadilly and Waterloo & City lines" until 11.59am. I cannot tell whether that is a later TfL update or Time Out's own summary, and tfl.gov.uk was not read. Fix: either say that Time Out reports a network-wide stop after 21:00 and no trains on those three lines on Friday morning, attributed to Time Out, or check TfL's live status page before publishing. As it stands a reader could plan a Thursday evening journey from the draft.
2. **"began the first of eight strikes" (summary and In short).** No source says it began. PN-259 and Time Out give the plan; the RMT's 26 November release says action is "starting today". Fix: "was due to begin at 12:00" or attribute "began" to the RMT's "starting today", unless a source dated after 12:00 on 26 November is added.
3. **Time Out's title and nature.** The source line reads "London Tube Strikes Winter and Christmas 2026/2027: Full list of dates"; the page's title is "London Tube Strikes Winter 2026/2027: Full List of Dates and Travel Advice Today (November 2026)" (the words "Winter and Christmas" are from its address). It is a rolling page, updated by day, so what we read will change; saying "as read on 26 November at about 16:00 UTC" would help.
4. **Small, an omission:** the RMT's survey release says the union wrote to TfL that it "remains available for talks despite TfL claims in the media to the contrary". The draft gives the first half only. For a neutral piece, one clause on that disagreement would be fair.

Checks out: all eight dates and times (TfL PN-259 and both RMT pages and Time Out agree: 26 November, 17 and 19 December, 4 and 6 January, 17, 23 and 25 February, each from 12:00 for 24 hours); PN-259 is dated Monday 23 November 2026; "the first in a series of dates announced by the RMT between November 2026 and February 2027" word for word; the first strike's plan as TfL gave it on 23 November (normal until about 10:00, none on the Circle or Piccadilly lines all day, none on the Waterloo & City after midday, Friday start from about 07:00, normal by about 18:00); the Elizabeth line, DLR, Overground, buses and trams; the dispute as TfL describes it (voluntary four-day week, five-day option with a guarantee of the same depot, fatigue tools, 35 hours' pay, ASLEF has accepted); both Nick Dent quotes word for word; the RMT's account (so-called voluntary four-day week, Trains Modernisation Framework, more driving time, longer duties, earlier starts and later finishes, short-notice shift changes, transfer rights and annual leave, displacement from depots, job cuts and the Bakerloo rosters, ACAS talks, strike action suspended three times); Eddie Dempsey's two quotes word for word; the survey (over 700 frontline staff, conducted in November 2026, two in three want the Mayor to step in, the letter to TfL); the ASLEF organiser's words as told to the BBC, and that later strikes may be called off.

Not checked: that there is no public journeys figure and no statement from the Mayor or the Department for Transport (a negative I cannot prove); the BBC report (the draft says it did not read it either); tfl.gov.uk (refused to the journalist).
